# Content Calendar — 60 posting ideas

Starting **August 2026**. Twelve weeks, five ideas a week, sequenced against the Indian
financial calendar so each post lands when people are already thinking about it.

**Realistic cadence: 3 posts a week, not 5.** You're part-time. The extra two are there so a
missed day doesn't break the run. Consistency beats volume — an advisor posting twice a week for
a year beats one posting daily for a month and stopping.

---

## The rules (read once)

Content is where compliance quietly goes wrong, because it feels casual. It isn't.

- **Never state or imply a return.** No "12% returns", no "double your money", no screenshots of a
  fund's past performance as a hook.
- **Never name a scheme as a recommendation.** Talk about the *category* or the *decision*.
- **Put ARN-267891 in your profile bio**, not in every post. That satisfies disclosure without
  making each post read like a disclaimer.
- **Insurance posts must name the insurer** if a specific product is described. Generic education
  about cover types doesn't need it.
- **Never post a client's details, portfolio or story** without written permission — and even then,
  anonymise. "A client in Thane" is fine; anything identifiable is not.
- **Tax content: say "as per current rules" and add "check with your CA".** Tax law changes and
  varies by person.
- **Don't argue with people in comments.** Answer once, factually, then move to DM.

## Where to post

**LinkedIn first.** Your BARC, TCS, Morgan Stanley, HDFC/Axis/Kotak clusters are all there, and
professional finance content performs. Post natively — no external links in the post body.

**WhatsApp Status second.** Your existing clients and warm contacts see it with zero algorithm in
the way. Same content, cropped.

Skip Instagram and YouTube for now. They need volume and production you don't have time for.

---

## The only two metrics that matter

Not likes. Track:

1. **DMs received** — the actual conversion event
2. **Profile views** — whether the content reaches the right people

If a post gets 200 likes and no DMs, it entertained people. If it gets 12 likes and 2 DMs, it worked.

## CTA rotation

Never end every post with "DM me". Rotate:

- Nothing at all (roughly half your posts — this is what builds credibility)
- "Happy to send the one-page Financial Organiser if useful — just say so."
- "If this raised a question about your own situation, my DMs are open."
- "I run a 45-minute session on this for teams and societies. Ask if you'd like the outline."

That last one is how a post becomes a **FinRakshak session at BARC or a Thane society** — which
is the highest-leverage outcome available to you, because session attendees are first-party
consented leads with no DPDP question at all.
